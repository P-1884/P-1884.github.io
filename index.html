Philip Holloway

Research Fellow in Strong Gravitational Lensing

I am a research fellow based at the Institute of Cosmology and Gravitation (University of Portsmouth, UK). My work focuses on strong gravitational lensing, the deflection of light due to gravity as it passes massive objects such as galaxies, galaxy groups or clusters.

My recent work (see below) has focussed on Bayesian inference, cosmology, and strong lens finding. I am a member of the Euclid and LSST Collaborations alongside Space Warps, a citizen science collaboration which aims to identify strong gravitational lenses with the help of volunteer members of the public.

I also have experience with public outreach, and am always happy to talk about my work and astrophysics in general - if you're organising an event and looking for a speaker, I'd love to hear from you.

Selected Publications

  • Lens Modeling and Cosmological Inference from an Impure Sample of Galaxy-Galaxy Strong Lenses
    Holloway, P., Verma, A., Marshall, P. J., et al. (2026, MNRAS accepted), arXiv:2609.21699

    This paper asks two questions. How well can we measure lens parameters in LSST data, and what can we do with the 90% of LSST lenses for which we won't have spectroscopic confirmation.
    Answers: 1) 3.7% precision in Einstein radius is achievable 2) Cosmology!

  • Euclid Quick Data Release (Q1): The Strong Lensing Discovery Engine A – System overview and first lens sample
    Euclid Collaboration: Walmsley, M., Holloway, P., Lines, N. E. P., et al. (2026), A&A, 711, A26

    This paper presented 500 high-grade lenses identified in Euclid Q1 data, a 63deg region of sky. At the time, this was the largest catalogue of strong lenses identified from space-based data.

  • Euclid Quick Data Release (Q1): The Strong Lensing Discovery Engine E – Ensemble classification of strong gravitational lenses: lessons for Data Release 1
    Euclid Collaboration: Holloway, P., Verma, A., Walmsley, M., et al. (2026), A&A, 711, A30

    Following the Q1 discoveries, this paper looks to the future, asking what lens purity is achievable when combining Euclid lens classifiers. We find an ensemble approach provides significant improvement.

  • A Bayesian approach to strong lens finding in the era of wide-area surveys
    Holloway, P., Marshall, P. J., Verma, A., et al. (2024), MNRAS, 530, 1297

    Strong lens classifiers are numerous but often vary in performance. This paper presents a Bayesian formalism to combine multiple classifiers and produce calibrated probabilities that a system is a strong lens.

  • On the detectability of strong lensing in near-infrared surveys
    Holloway, P., Verma, A., Marshall, P. J., More, A., Tecza, M. (2023), MNRAS

    This paper asks - are there any lenses detectable in JWST surveys? The answer is a resounding yes, which has been supported by recent spectacular discoveries (e.g. COWLS II).

  • Strong gravitational lenses in the era of wide-field surveys
    Holloway, P. (2025), PhD thesis, University of Oxford

    My thesis studies a wide range of strong lens science from cosmological inference to lens finding.
